01 / THE IDEA

Our planet is full
of patterns.

Can a pretrained image model recognize those patterns when every image has thirteen spectral channels instead of three?

Satellite scenes contain information about agriculture, forests, water, industry, and settlement. Automatically labeling these regions can support large-scale environmental monitoring and land-cover analysis.

We adapted an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 to process the 13-band EuroSAT dataset, then trained a custom classifier using progressive fine-tuning. We evaluated it on the test split and examined which land types it confuses.

WHAT TRANSFERRED?

Keep the visual knowledge. Change the interface.

We replaced ResNet-50's original 3-channel convolution with a new 13-channel layer and replaced its final classification layer with an identity mapping. The remaining pretrained residual backbone feeds a three-layer head.

In the latest notebook, the 13-channel convolution is initialized using pretrained RGB filters for the visible bands and a scaled mean filter for the other bands. It is then trained on multispectral data.

06 / WHAT'S NEXT

There’s more to
the picture.

The project demonstrates effective transfer learning from everyday imagery to multispectral remote sensing, while the confusion matrix shows exactly where broader context and additional evaluation could help.

01 / STRENGTH

Water-body scenes are especially well distinguished: 357 of 359 SeaLake samples in the new test were correctly classified.

02 / CHALLENGE

Pasture has the lowest recall (91.0%), with Highway (92.0%) and Permanent Crop (92.4%) also leaving room for improvement.

03 / NEXT EXPERIMENT

The measured accuracy is 95.72% at Gaussian-noise strength 0.2 and 60.31% at strength 0.8. Next: investigate sensitivity to 30° and 45° rotations.

Evaluation provenance

The evaluation and robustness results come from ResNetModel(7).ipynb, which loads Model State Archives/96.6%Acc.pth: 96.56% clean test accuracy, 0.1289 loss, 2,759 test images. The confusion matrix represents 2,664 correct predictions; macro F1 is calculated from the matrix. The 52 recorded fine-tuning epochs came from the copied first 20 epoch logs plus 32 preserved notebook output epochs; the chart omits Stage I epochs 11–20 after its minimum validation loss at epoch 10; epoch 31 records the lowest validation loss (0.1280). The 12 frozen warm-up epochs have no preserved per-epoch metrics. The resilience panel uses the supplied transformation measurements, including four Gaussian-noise levels. Randomized perturbations can vary between runs. The 30 gallery images and five selected test-set misclassifications have separately exported model predictions and class-specific Grad-CAM maps.
